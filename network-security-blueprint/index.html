Purpose & scope

This blueprint defines the target-state security architecture for the enterprise network: perimeter, campus/branch, data center, hybrid cloud, remote access and OT. It turns the NSA guide's device-level best practices into enterprise controls you can measure, and it adds Zero Trust and cloud-native extensions the NSA guide leaves out of scope (the guide covers existing networks and says it may need to change as Zero Trust matures).

  • In scope: routers, switches, firewalls, VPN/ZTNA gateways, wireless, cloud network constructs, network management plane, OT network boundaries.
  • Out of scope: endpoint hardening, application security and IAM governance, except where they act as network policy decision points.
  • Control sources: NSA NISG §2–§10 (device and architecture), CNSSP 15 / NIST SP 800-52r2 (cryptography), OMB M-21-31 (log retention), enterprise extensions (ZT, cloud, OT).

Design principles

  1. Defense in depth, vendor diversity. Use layered NGFWs from different vendors so one unpatched vulnerability can't defeat every layer (§2.1).
  2. Deny by default, permit by exception. Apply this at every boundary and log every deny (§2.4).
  3. Segment by function and trust. Users, servers, printers/IoT, voice and OT each go in their own zone (§2.2).
  4. No backdoors. Multi-homed devices only bridge networks of equal trust (§2.3).
  5. Authenticate everything that connects. Use 802.1X and certificates rather than MAC-based trust (§2.5).
  6. Isolated, encrypted, centrally controlled management plane. Use AAA, SSHv2 and SNMPv3 (§4, §7).
  7. Assume breach. Redundant, tamper-resistant logs, synchronized time and full visibility (§6).
  8. Identity and context over location. Move from broad VPN to ZTNA and microsegmentation (Ext).

Layered defense model

L1 · Edge & Perimeter
Border routers with uRPF and ingress/egress ACLs, DDoS scrubbing, dual-vendor NGFW with IPS, a DMZ for public services and outbound proxies, and VPN gateways locked down to IKE/ESP.
§2.1 §2.4 §2.6 §8.2
L2 · Segmentation
VLANs/VRFs by function, internal firewalls between trust zones, east-west microsegmentation, and an OT IDMZ.
§2.2 §2.3 Ext
L3 · Access Control
802.1X NAC with certificates, port security as a fallback, no DTP, native VLAN isolation, unused ports shut down, and ZTNA for remote users.
§2.5 §9
L4 · Control Plane
Authenticated OSPF/EIGRP/BGP (HMAC-SHA-384/512, unique keys per neighbor), no RIP, no source routing, and proxy ARP disabled.
§8 §9.6
L5 · Management Plane
OOB management network, management ACLs, centralized TACACS+/RADIUS (×2), least privilege, MFA, SSHv2, TLS 1.2+, SNMPv3 authPriv, and unnecessary services disabled.
§4 §5 §7
L6 · Integrity & Lifecycle
Image hash verification, configuration change control and drift detection, current vendor-supported OS/hardware, and EoL replacement.
§3
L7 · Visibility & Response
Syslog to ≥2 collectors and SIEM, NIDS/NDR and packet capture, authenticated NTP in UTC, login success/failure logging, and accounting records.
